Features

  • Stereo 8-bit two’s complement output at 15,734.375 Hz, tick-locked to the raster (262.5 ticks per field)
  • Two DMA-fed streams, as in video: SAMPLES (PCM pairs) and AUDCMD (16-bit records: voice SETs, WAITs, CONFIGURE, program uploads)
  • Voices with 16-bit phase, signed 8-bit amplitude, left or right or mono; square, wavetable and SN76489-style LFSR noise
  • The tick routine is assembled from templates at CONFIGURE time and patched in place by SET: no per-voice dispatch
  • Tables live in the Z80’s RAM; a title may upload its own sound program and run it
  • PCM popped straight from its FIFO under the voices; a missed vblank holds the last pair, the voices keep sounding
  • No 68000-visible registers, no status path: deposits are scheduled against documented drain rates

Device Complement

  • ENGINE — existing descriptor DMA master; AUDIO_FIFO_W feeds SAMPLES, SPARE feeds AUDCMD; no change
  • 2×2 IDT7200L15 — SAMPLES pair (the rev-1 footprint) and AUDCMD pair, each ganged 16-bit; 7201 drops in
  • Z80B 6 MHz or Z80H 8 MHz (decided by §5) on its own oscillator can; 27C128 program + tables; 6264 8K×8 RAM
  • 74HC138 + half a 74HC139 decode; 74HC244 status port (/EF, /HF of both pairs)
  • 2× 74HC574 DAC latches driving the existing R-2R ladders, LM358 stage, 3× LM386 (rev-1 CD4049 inverters removed)
  • 12-pin debug header: /WR, /IORQ, DEBUG select, D0..D7, GND; DUMP records write a state snapshot to it
  • PORTS — rev-1 pop logic (AUDIO_CONTROL / AUDIO_STATUS) retires (package B)

Proposal revision. Rev 1 ships the SAMPLES path only (§3.1 rev 1). The Z80 form was decided on 2026-09-26 after the CPLD form fitted with no headroom (§7). The first Z80 ROM matched the tick law bit for bit but cost 1263 T per tick against 381 at 6 MHz; the second form on this page (templated tick, SET patching, PCM from its own FIFO, a status port, a minimal NMI) also matches bit for bit and costs 816 T, so no Z80 in the bin carries Smurf’s four voices (§5). The sound processor’s final form is an open decision (§7). PRELIMINARY marks what the model pins and the Z80 test is confirming; ADVANCE marks concepts; OPEN marks a question no gate has answered.

General Description1

The audio pipeline keeps the video pipeline’s shape, two DMA-fed streams and a consumer with no CPU bus, but the consumer is a small Z80 with its own ROM and RAM. SAMPLES is the data plane, one {L, R} pair per tick, popped straight from its FIFO. AUDCMD is the control plane: 16-bit records that set voice parameters, wait a number of ticks, configure the tick routine, or upload a program. All voice state and every table live in the Z80’s RAM, so the 68000 decides what the machine is by what it loads, and it never touches a sound register: it writes records into RAM and points a descriptor at them, exactly as it does for VIDCMD.

Mechanisms3

Status stamps: PRODUCTION = built, verified; on rev-1 boards for the rev-1 SAMPLES path. PRELIMINARY = law pinned in mixer/mixer.h and griffin.yml, Z80 program under measurement. ADVANCE = concept, specification may change.

3.1 Two streams — SAMPLES and AUDCMD PRELIMINARY

ENGINE deposits one word per strobe, at most 32 words per descriptor, from anywhere in RAM: AUDIO_FIFO_W into the SAMPLES pair, SPARE into the AUDCMD pair. The tick pops one SAMPLES pair, left byte then right, after reading the status port; if the pair is empty it holds the last one, so a missed vblank is a 16 ms hold and playback resumes on the next deposit. The main loop drains AUDCMD records in order in the gap between ticks, batching 128 blind when the status port says half full. A write to a full 7200 is ignored, so the author keeps each queue inside its depth against the §5 drain rates. Control and data never block each other. Rev 1 PRODUCTION: the same SAMPLES pair, PORTS pops both chips on every tick while AUDIO_CONTROL.ENABLE is set; that path stays in griffin.yml as rev-1-only until package B.

CONFIGURE rebuilds the tick: the drain points the JP at a hold stub that re-outputs the last pair (the NMI cannot be masked), fills the tick buffer from ROM templates, and points the JP back; about 2000 to 3000 T once, and the voice phases survive because they live in fixed RAM words. SET patches the live code with single-instruction stores, so a tick can land between any two instructions without tearing. The law is Mixer::tick() of mixer/mixer.h, the model that passed the SN76489 A/B, and the Z80 program is compared to it bit for bit.

3.5 PSG driver — nine bytes a frame become a dozen SETs PRELIMINARY (model)

mixer/psg_driver consumes the same byte stream an SN76489 would and emits SETs for voice bytes that changed: period to INC through a 1024-entry table, attenuation to AMP through the 2 dB ladder, noise control to the noise voice’s INC and RESET_PHASE. Over the Smurf playthrough: 0.93 SETs per field mean, 12 max, 17 on the first frame. A PSG port sends one CONFIGURE at start (three square voices and one noise voice, mono) and SETs thereafter.

3.6 Uploaded programs — a title brings its own chip ADVANCE

LOAD places two bytes per word anywhere in RAM with a 16-bit sum; JUMP points the tick JP at the image. The NMI vector stays in ROM. An uploaded SN76489 or AY-3-8910 at the register-write level makes the 68000 side of a port a byte forwarder. FM chips do not fit at any bin clock.

3.7 Limits PRELIMINARY (measured on the model)

A 16-bit INC gives at most one LFSR shift per tick; the SN76489 reaches 1.8 per sample at rate 3 clocked from tone 2 at period 4, so in Smurf’s jump tail periodic noise tops out at 1049 Hz where the chip reaches 1864 Hz. Listening found it unobjectionable. Voice count and stereo trade against the tick budget; the sample rate does not move.

Application Notes4

Costs are bus words per field (ENGINE delivery) and 68000 cycles per field. The frame budget reference is 233,625 SYSCLK per field.

PSG game port — three tones and noise, SN76489-class1 CONFIGURE, then ≤12 SETs + WAITs per field · a few hundred cyclesPRELIMINARY (model)

Recipe: CONFIGURE 4 voices mono, no PCM; the game’s PSG bytes go through psg_driver into a record buffer; one descriptor per field points at it, then a WAIT to the next field boundary. Queue field k+1’s records during field k (RT3).

Notes: the Smurf port. Or forward the raw PSG bytes to an uploaded chip emulation (§3.6).

PCM soundtrack or one-shots — the SAMPLES stream≈263 words/field per stereo stream · CPU copies or rendersPRELIMINARY

Recipe: a sample buffer in RAM; the display list deposits it in bursts of at most 32 words spread across the field (Smurf’s nine bursts), exactly as rev 1 does. CONFIGURE with PCM on; it mixes under the voices for the cost of one pop.

Notes: a missed vblank holds the last pair for the rest of that field and resumes; no re-priming.

Tracker / chiptune playback — song as a record list≈3–20 words per row · zero cycles while playingADVANCE

Recipe: a whole song is SET/WAIT words in RAM; ENGINE streams it with count-32 descriptors as the FIFO drains. WAIT counts are tick-exact and raster-locked.

Wavetable instruments and drums — LOAD + CONFIGUREa table is LOAD words once; a note is 3 SETsADVANCE

Recipe: LOAD single-cycle waves at 256-byte pages (pre-scaled copies for volume); CONFIGURE the voice as a table voice on its page; SET AMP selects the page thereafter. A drum is a page played once by an INC chosen so the phase runs it out, then AMP off.

Stereo placementa side per voice, baked at CONFIGUREPRELIMINARY

Recipe: each voice is left or right (one add each); mono content sets the global mono flag and every voice costs one add into one accumulator written to both DACs. PCM is always stereo.

Notes: period-exact: 1984 hardware panned by enable bits or not at all; per-voice L/R levels arrived with the PC Engine in 1987.

Authoring Rules and Limitations6

6.1 Rules of thumb for a sound driver PRELIMINARY (model)

#Rule of thumb
RT1RESET, CONFIGURE, then SET. RAM is not reset. CONFIGURE the instrument once per screen or title (voice count, modes, sides, PCM, mono); SET the notes. A SET to a structural field waits for the next CONFIGURE.
RT2A note is one descriptor. Three words, SET INC_LO, SET INC_HI, SET AMP, delivered by ENGINE from RAM. Only send bytes that changed.
RT3WAIT is the clock; queue a field ahead. A WAIT that expires on an empty FIFO re-syncs to whenever the next words arrive, so deliver field k+1’s records during field k.
RT4Schedule, don’t poll. There is no readback. Place deposits at lines so neither queue exceeds its depth against the §5 drain rates. PCM has its own FIFO and never delays a record.
RT5Budget amplitude, not the chip. Saturation happens on every add. Keep the sum of active peaks under 127 per channel; at a per-voice peak of 31 that is four voices.
RT6Pitch is exact enough; do not table-correct it. Every PSG period lands within 2 cents. Sweeps update INC once per field or once per row.
RT7Long samples through SAMPLES, short waves through LOAD. A table voice loops a 256-byte page; anything longer streams through the SAMPLES FIFO under the voices.

6.2 Rules and limitations PRELIMINARY

#Rule
AR1Each pair is written only by its ENGINE strobe; a write to a full 7200 is ignored. The author keeps each queue inside 256 (512 with 7201s) by construction.
AR2SAMPLES is popped once per tick; an empty pair holds the last sample until the next deposit. Deposit at least twice per field for continuous PCM, as on rev 1.
AR3Records execute in the gap, in order, before the next tick’s mix; how many fit in one gap is the §5 drain rate. Records beyond it execute next gap; a CONFIGURE holds the output for its rebuild time.
AR4SETs execute in queue order, so a later PHASE SET wins over RESET_PHASE, and a SET queued before a boundary is heard in that tick.
AR5One LFSR; which voice steps it is a CONFIGURE field; other noise voices read the same register; a shift is heard one tick after the carry.
AR6A noise voice shifts at most once per tick (§3.7).
AR7Nothing in RAM is reset by /RS; the ROM initialises its own state and parks the DACs at midscale. Uploaded programs and tables are gone after a reset and must be LOADed again.
AR8SET reaches only the 128-byte window. Tables and programs enter through LOAD; CONFIGURE tells a voice which page it plays.
AR9A JUMP after a LOAD whose sum failed is refused. A hung upload is silence until the system reset; there is no watchdog.
AR10Nothing on the 68000 side observes the Z80. Development uses the emulator; silicon debugging uses DUMP records and the header.
