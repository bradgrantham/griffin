Features

  • Four 8-bit channels, two per side (0 and 1 left, 2 and 3 right), each a FIFO read straight into its own R-2R ladder
  • Per-channel sample rate from an 8-bit PERIOD: 1,573,437.5 / (PERIOD + 1) Hz, 6.15 kHz to well above 100 kHz; PERIOD 99 is the 15,734.375 Hz tick exactly
  • Pitch = rate / table length, both chosen by the 68000; tables loop by re-pointing descriptors
  • 6-bit linear volume per channel as a duty cycle on an analog switch, 64 steps, averaged by the output stage
  • An empty channel is silent, not a DC step; no per-voice arithmetic anywhere
  • DMA-only: samples and control records arrive through ENGINE; no 68000 bus interface, no readback
  • Smurf’s SN76489 soundtrack costs ENGINE 0.97 % of a frame on the model, 0 underruns

Device Complement

  • ENGINE: existing descriptor DMA master, unchanged; the AUDIO_FIFO_W strobe carries every audio deposit
  • 74HC138: enabled by the strobe, decodes bits 10:8 of each word into one FIFO’s /W
  • 5× IDT7200 (256×9, byte wide): four sample FIFOs and one control FIFO
  • AUDIO: ATF1508AS on the 25.175 MHz pixel clock; period counters, volume gates, control reader (120/128 LC)
  • 4× 4610X-R2R-103LF: 8-bit R-2R ladders, one per channel, driven by the FIFO Q buses
  • 1× 74HC4066: one switch per channel between its ladder and its side’s summing node (HC preferred over CD4066 for on-resistance)
  • 1× LM358: an inverting summer per side, virtual ground biased at 2.5 V by a shared 10k/10k divider with 10 µF; per side 2k ∥ 8.2 nF feedback, 4.7k to GND, 100 µF coupling to LINE

General Description1

The chip does no arithmetic on samples. Each channel is a FIFO whose output bus drives its own resistor ladder, and the AUDIO CPLD only decides when each FIFO advances and how much of the time each ladder is connected to the output. The display list is the loop hardware: the 68000 prepares waveform tables in RAM at load time and ENGINE descriptors deposit them, byte by byte, a few words per line. Pitch is the channel’s rate divided by the table’s length. Volume is Paula’s mechanism, a duty cycle: the switch between a ladder and its side’s summing node is closed for VOLUME/64 of every 49.17 kHz frame, and the output stage averages it. Registers arrive through a fifth FIFO as two-byte records, so the whole subsystem stays DMA-only.

3.1 Deposit path: the destination rides in the word PRELIMINARY

Every audio byte is one 16-bit word deposited by ENGINE on the AUDIO_FIFO_W strobe. A 74HC138 enabled by that strobe decodes the word’s bits 10:8 into one FIFO’s /W, and the low byte is what that FIFO stores. Destinations 0 to 3 are the sample channels, 4 is the control FIFO; 5 to 7 are not assigned. The ENGINE RTL and its descriptor format do not change: a descriptor is a run of words, at most 32, and a run to one destination is simply words that all carry the same tag. The tag-in-the-word decode was chosen on 2026-09-27 over adding a destination field to ENGINE’s descriptor.

3.2 Channel clock and pop PRELIMINARY

Each channel has an 8-bit PERIOD and an 8-bit down counter COUNT. At every step, if COUNT is 0 the channel pops and COUNT reloads from PERIOD; otherwise COUNT decrements. A pop is a one-clock high pulse on the FIFO’s /R, one clock after the step’s counter edge. /R is otherwise held low, so the FIFO’s Q bus always shows the current sample and drives the ladder directly. Steps are exactly 50 per line, so the pop pattern is locked to the raster in rate.

3.3 Volume gate: a binary rate multiplier PRELIMINARY

GATE[n] closes the 74HC4066 switch between ladder n and its side’s summing node. Within each 64-tick frame the gate is on in six disjoint windows of the free-running timebase, one per VOLUME bit, with duties 1/2 down to 1/64. Tick 0 is in no window, so the gate is on for exactly VOLUME ticks of 64 and the average is exactly VOLUME/64. The residue sits at multiples of 49.17 kHz, as with plain PWM. The gate is a registered output, so the switch never sees decode glitches.

The gate is forced off while the channel’s FIFO is empty, so an empty channel contributes nothing rather than holding a DC level, and during the pop clock, when Q is high-impedance. The summing node sits at the 2.5 V midscale, so a ladder at 0x80 injects no current whether its switch is open or closed, and a volume change causes no DC step (§3.6).

3.4 Control records PRELIMINARY

Register byteValue byteEffect
0..38 bitsPERIOD of channel n
4..7low 6 bitsVOLUME of channel n − 4

The reader takes a byte in four clocks whenever the control FIFO is not empty (/R low two clocks, high one), so a record is consumed within 8 clocks of arriving. Two records complete in the FIFO at a step’s first clock are both in force at that step; a third lands one step later. A record split by an empty FIFO waits for its second byte. Only the low three bits of the register byte are decoded.

3.5 Reset and the empty FIFO PRELIMINARY

Reset clears PERIOD, COUNT and VOLUME to 0 and holds every /R high, as the 7200 requires while its /RS is low. An unconfigured channel therefore pops every step and plays silently at VOLUME 0: set PERIOD before depositing samples. A pop of an empty FIFO is ignored by the part and the gate is off; a write to a full FIFO is dropped. Nothing can be read back.

3.6 Analog stage OPEN

Per side, two ladders feed the virtual-ground node of an LM358 inverting summer through their 74HC4066 switches (control = GATE[n]). The summer’s non-inverting input is biased at 2.5 V by a 10k/10k divider with a 10 µF capacitor, shared by both sides. A ladder at midscale (0x80 = 2.5 V) injects zero current whether its switch is closed or open, so volume changes cause no DC step, and each channel’s contribution is independent of the other switch’s state, which a passive sum would not give. Feedback is 2k in parallel with 8.2 nF (the 9.7 kHz corner, which averages the switching), then 4.7k to GND, 100 µF coupling to LINE. Full scale is ±0.625 V per channel at the summer, ±1.25 V for two, about 2.5 V. There is no clamp.

This stage has not been breadboarded. The open risk is the 4066’s on-resistance and switching time at low volumes, where the gate is on for one to a few 8-clock ticks per frame.

Application Notes4

Costs are ENGINE words per frame and share of the 233,625-SYSCLK frame. PRELIMINARY recipes have run on the host model; ADVANCE recipes are derived from the laws and not yet run.

PSG port: three tones and noise, SN76489-classSmurf: 364 words, 153 descriptors per frame mean · 0.97 %PRELIMINARY (model)

Recipe: tone c plays on channel c as a square table of LENGTH samples at PERIOD, chosen so 1,573,437.5 / (PERIOD + 1) / LENGTH lands within a cent of the tone’s 3,579,545 / (32 N) where (PERIOD + 1) × LENGTH allows; tables are cached by length. Noise plays on channel 3 as the shift register’s output sequence stored as a table (white: the 15-bit LFSR, period 32,767; periodic: 15), each bit repeated when the shift rate is below the 6.15 kHz floor, capped at 65,536 samples. Attenuation a becomes VOLUME round(63 × 10−a/10), 15 becomes 0. On a frequency change the new table starts at the old one’s phase fraction.

Notes: tones at or above 7,867 Hz (N ≤ 14) are silenced, as the reference band-limits them. The ladder gives 63, 50, 40, 32, 25, 20, 16, 13, 10, 8, 6, 5, 4, 3, 3, 0, so attenuations 13 and 14 coincide. Smurf used 33 tables, 3.7 KB as words. Code: audio/psg_channels.h.

Scheduling and Measured Constants5

The 68000 has no readback, so it mirrors the chip. Over one line, a channel with COUNT c and PERIOD p at the line’s first step pops 1 + (49 − c) / (p + 1) times if c < 50 (integer division) and none otherwise, and leaves p − (49 − last pop step) for the next line. The scheduler runs this per line and knows every FIFO’s fill exactly; over 200,000 random lines, including two PERIOD records per line, the closed form equals the chip.

Authoring Rules and Limitations6

6.1 Rules of thumb for a sound driver PRELIMINARY (model)

#Rule of thumb
RT1Set PERIOD before depositing. After reset every channel pops every step, so samples deposited before PERIOD is set drain almost at once.
RT2Burst every 8 lines, staggered by channel. Each burst tops the FIFO up to the pops expected over the next 8 lines plus a margin of 2, less the current fill. A stream that starts bursts at the very next line instead of waiting for its slot. If a faster PERIOD is queued, predict with it, so an upward sweep cannot drain the FIFO.
RT3Mirror, don’t poll. Run the closed-form pop law (§5) per line; it is exact, so the fill is known without readback.
RT4Loop tables with descriptors. A table is played by re-pointing descriptors at it, at most 32 words each; a table never needs to fit in the 256-byte FIFO.
RT5Pitch comes from table length. Pick PERIOD and LENGTH together. Most pitches land within a cent; high tones need (PERIOD + 1) × LENGTH to hit an integer, and the misses cluster above about 4.6 kHz (Smurf’s worst: 8.15 cents at 7.46 kHz). Fine modulation comes from switching tables at cycle boundaries or dithering PERIOD at frame rate.
RT6Budget levels per side. Two channels share a summing node with no clamp; keep the sum of their levels inside what the output stage takes.

6.2 Rules and limitations PRELIMINARY

#Rule
AR1Each FIFO is 256 deep. A write when full is dropped (samples lost); a pop when empty is silent. The author keeps every FIFO between the two by construction.
AR2A PERIOD change applies to the samples popped after it. Place its record at the first line by which every sample already queued has been popped, so the new rate applies to the new table and not the old tail (resolution: one line).
AR3VOLUME records are placed at the next line and apply from the step they complete in.
AR4At most two records land per step; a third waits one step. Records are two deposits each to destination 4.
AR5Latency from a table change to the speaker is the burst wait (up to 8 lines, 0.25 ms) plus the queued fill (pops per burst + 2 samples): under 1 ms by the README’s derivation; the log estimates about 8 ticks (0.5 ms) for Smurf.
AR6PERIOD is 8 bits: 6.15 kHz is the slowest rate. Below it, repeat samples in the table.
AR7Samples are offset binary, 0x80 silent. An empty FIFO is silent too, so a channel can simply be allowed to run dry to stop.
AR8Nothing on the 68000 side can observe the chip. Development uses the model (and, when built, the emulator); the scheduler’s mirror is the only fill gauge.

The model and testbench align each line start with a step boundary. On hardware the phase between the raster and the AUDIO timebase is whatever reset leaves; I believe the margin of 2 absorbs the difference, but no source checks it.
